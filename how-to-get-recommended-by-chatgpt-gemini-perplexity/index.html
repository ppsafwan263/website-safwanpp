Introduction: search has changed, and so should you

A few years ago, ranking on Google's first page was the finish line for most businesses. That finish line has moved. Millions of people now ask ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity a question and get a single, direct answer, often with no clicking, no scrolling, and no list of ten blue links to compare.

This shift has a name: Generative Engine Optimization (GEO), sometimes also called Answer Engine Optimization (AEO). Instead of trying to rank first among ten results, you're now trying to be one of the three or four sources an AI model chooses to mention by name. If your business isn't in that short list, you simply don't exist in that conversation, no matter how good your website looks.

This isn't mysterious or unfair. AI systems have clear, learnable patterns in what they choose to cite and recommend, and small and mid-sized businesses, including local businesses across Kerala and the rest of India, can compete here in ways that were much harder in classic Google SEO.

This guide walks you through what changed, why it changed, and what you can do, in plain language, to help your business become easier to discover and understand across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity in 2026.

Why this matters right now

Search behaviour is shifting. Many Google searches end without a click because an answer appears directly on the results page. At the same time, people use AI assistants for product questions, service questions, and "what's the best X for Y" questions that used to go straight to Google.

Here's what makes this worth paying attention to for business owners and marketers in Kerala and across India:

  • AI answers are winner-take-few. A traditional search results page may show many links. An AI answer may name only a handful of sources. Missing that shortlist can mean missing a potential customer's consideration.
  • Recommendations can reach decision-ready people. Someone asking an assistant to compare providers may already be researching a purchase or service.
  • Visibility can compound. Businesses that make useful, accurate information available across their site and credible third-party sources give people—and AI systems—more context to discover them.
  • This is becoming a real discipline. Research into how brands appear in AI answers is growing. Focus on useful content and verifiable signals, rather than treating GEO as a guaranteed formula.
  • India's digital audience is changing quickly. Consumers from Kochi to Bengaluru to Delhi have more ways to research services, including AI chat tools alongside search engines.

What makes AI search different from Google search

Traditional search engines crawl and index pages, then rank them to give users links to evaluate. AI answer engines can retrieve information from selected sources, interpret it, and generate a synthesized answer in their own words, sometimes citing a handful of sources.

Your content is not only competing to rank; it also needs to be understandable, relevant, and trustworthy to a system interpreting it. If a page is vague, poorly organized, or hard to parse, it may be less useful to both readers and answer systems.

ChatGPT

ChatGPT can browse the live web for some questions. Clear answers and well-structured pages can make information easier to interpret, but no particular format guarantees citation.

Perplexity

Perplexity behaves like a research assistant: it retrieves information from multiple sources and visibly cites them. Fresh, detailed, original content can give readers useful evidence to explore.

Gemini

Gemini is connected to Google's broader search ecosystem. Traditional SEO fundamentals—helpful content, technical accessibility, and a usable mobile experience—remain important groundwork.

Understanding these differences can help you prioritize effort instead of trying to do everything for every platform at once.

The foundation: E-E-A-T still runs the show

Google's E-E-A-T framework—Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness—remains a useful way to think about the quality and credibility of your content. Clear evidence for these qualities can also help readers evaluate your business and your work.

Experience

Show that real people with first-hand experience created the content. Use specific examples, before-and-after outcomes, and details grounded in work you have actually done.

Expertise

Make the person or team behind the content clear. Author bios, professional backgrounds, certifications, and relevant experience give readers context about who is sharing the advice.

Authoritativeness

Build a reputation over time. Credible sites linking to you, industry coverage, comparison articles, and accurate mentions across the web can help establish your business as a known entity.

Trustworthiness

Be accurate, transparent, and consistent. Clear contact information, honest pricing, visible reviews, a secure website, and claims you can support all help people assess your business.

Practical steps to get recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini & Perplexity

Write in an answer-first format

For every important page or post, start each section with a direct one- or two-sentence answer to the implied question, then explain further underneath. Avoid starting paragraphs with vague pronouns like "it," "this," or "they" without context; unclear references make content harder for readers and systems to follow.

Structure content with headers, lists, and tables

Organize information so readers can scan it and find an answer. Use descriptive H2 and H3 headings that match real questions, numbered steps for processes, bullet points for comparisons, and simple tables for pricing or specifications. Clear structure can reduce ambiguity.

Publish original data and insights

Generic advice is easy to find everywhere. Original research—even a small customer survey, an internal case study, or a useful statistic from your own business—gives readers something distinctive to learn from and gives other sources something specific to reference.

Keep content fresh

Review important pages regularly. Refresh outdated statistics and current pricing when they change, and show a "last updated" date when it helps readers understand the information's recency. A practical schedule is to revisit key pages every 60 to 90 days.

Add structured data (schema markup)

Schema markup is code that describes facts about your content to search engines. JSON-LD is a commonly used format. Depending on your business and page, structured data may describe your organization, local business, products or services, or FAQs.

For businesses in Kerala or elsewhere in India, make sure LocalBusiness details such as name, location, and service area are accurate. Structured data helps describe a page; it does not guarantee a ranking or AI citation.

Set up an llms.txt file

Some site owners publish an llms.txt file at their website root to point to useful pages. It is an optional emerging convention, not a required step or a proven way to secure AI citations. Prioritize accessible pages, a sitemap, and helpful content first.

Earn mentions on third-party sites

Independent references can give people more context about your business. Build a genuine presence on relevant review platforms, industry blogs, news sites, directories, and comparison pages.

  • Get listed and keep information consistent across relevant directories, including Google Business Profile and India-specific directories.
  • Pursue guest posts or expert quotes on respected industry sites.
  • Help journalists and bloggers with useful, accurate information when they cover your niche.
  • Participate in relevant communities where your expertise can help people.

Manage reviews and sentiment actively

For local recommendations, reviews can help people understand a business's reputation and customer experience. Ask customers for honest, detailed reviews without incentives, and respond to feedback promptly and professionally.

Don't abandon traditional SEO

GEO does not replace SEO. Fast-loading pages, mobile-friendly design, clean site architecture, accessible content, and relevant references remain useful foundations for discovery.

Track your AI visibility

Periodically ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity realistic questions your customers might ask, such as "best [service] in [city]." Record whether your business appears and which competitors or sources are mentioned. Treat the results as directional: answers can vary by query, location, time, and platform.

A simple monthly routine

Keep this manageable with a lightweight schedule:

Weekly

Ask AI assistants a handful of questions in your niche and note which sources are cited.

Monthly

Review analytics for referral visits from services such as chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, or gemini.google.com.

Quarterly

Refresh your highest-value pages, update facts, and review structured data for errors.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Keyword-stuffing over clarity. Repeated keywords make content harder to read; explain the topic clearly instead.
  • Ignoring third-party mentions. A useful website is important, but credible independent references can help establish context and reputation.
  • Letting content go stale. Outdated pricing or statistics can mislead readers and weaken trust.
  • Overlooking technical basics. Broken pages, inaccessible content, errors in structured data, slow load times, or poor mobile design can undermine the experience.
  • Chasing every platform equally. Prioritize based on where your customers actually research. B2B audiences may use research-focused assistants; local customers may rely more on Google Search and Maps.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to get cited by AI search engines?

There is no guaranteed timeline. AI visibility can vary by platform, prompt, and location. Improve useful content and your wider online presence, then monitor results over time rather than expecting an exact number of weeks.

Is GEO the same thing as SEO?

No, but they overlap. SEO focuses on making pages discoverable in search results; GEO focuses on making a business and its information useful to AI-generated answers. Strong SEO fundamentals can support both.

Do I need a developer to add schema markup or an llms.txt file?

Not always. WordPress, Shopify, and Webflow may offer built-in settings or plugins for some structured data. An llms.txt file is a plain-text file, but it is optional. For complex sites, a developer can help implement and test changes safely.

Which platform should I focus on first: ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity?

Start with the tools your customers are most likely to use. Research-heavy audiences may use ChatGPT or Perplexity, while local and consumer-facing audiences may find businesses through Google's search ecosystem as well as AI assistants. Ask customers how they research before choosing a priority.

Can small businesses in Kerala or elsewhere in India compete with large brands?

Yes. A small business can make its expertise and local relevance clear through specific, useful information and a trustworthy online presence. There is no guarantee of being recommended, but these are practical improvements within a business's control.

Final thoughts

Getting recommended by ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity in 2026 isn't about tricking an algorithm. It's about becoming a source that's genuinely easy to trust, easy to understand, and easy to verify. That means clear, well-structured content, real expertise and experience visible on the page, a footprint of credible mentions across the web, and a website that's technically clean enough for machines to read confidently.

The businesses winning in this new landscape aren't necessarily the biggest ones. They're the ones that treat AI visibility as seriously as they once treated Google rankings, and start with consistent, useful work. The steps above give you a clear, realistic starting point.
