01. What is Google’s AI Search?

Google’s AI Overviews — formerly known as Search Generative Experience (SGE) — is a feature that places an AI-generated answer directly at the top of the search results page. Before a user ever sees your website, Google’s AI has already synthesised information from hundreds of sources and delivered a direct response.

This is a structural transformation, not a cosmetic one. Previously, Google’s role was to point users toward information. Now, Google often is the information. The implication for businesses is profound: the path from a search query to your website has become significantly more competitive.

Practical Example: A potential client in Kannur searches “best digital marketing strategies for local business.” Instead of seeing your website at the top, they see a four-paragraph AI answer. Only if they want to go deeper — or if your site is cited as a source — do they interact with your brand at all.

Having worked with local businesses and e-commerce brands across Kerala, I have seen firsthand how this shift affects enquiry volumes. The businesses that understand and adapt to AI search are generating more qualified leads. Those that ignore it are watching their organic traffic decline month on month.

02. How AI Search Actually Works

Understanding the mechanics of AI search is essential for any serious SEO strategy. Google’s AI draws on a Large Language Model (LLM) trained on vast amounts of web content. When a user submits a query, the AI does not simply retrieve a cached answer — it dynamically composes a response by evaluating multiple trusted sources simultaneously.

The critical insight is that the AI is highly selective about which sources it draws from. It prioritises content that demonstrates clear signals of quality, authority, and trustworthiness. Specifically, Google’s systems evaluate:

  • Topical depth and specificity — content that goes beyond surface-level information and demonstrates genuine subject-matter knowledge.
  • Authorship signals — identifiable, credentialed authors with verifiable expertise in the topic area.
  • Citation and link authority — whether the content is referenced by other trusted, high-authority domains.
  • Freshness and accuracy — content that is regularly updated and factually consistent with other trusted sources.
  • Structured clarity — well-organised content with clear headings, logical flow, and direct answers to the user’s intent.
“Google’s AI does not rank pages — it evaluates whether your entire content operation deserves to represent authoritative knowledge on a given subject. That is a fundamentally different standard.” — Safwan PP, Digital Marketing Strategist, ppsafwan.com

03. The Real Impact on Website Traffic

Let us be direct about the consequences, because many businesses are still underestimating the scale of this shift.

The Zero-Click Problem

A zero-click search occurs when a user finds their answer directly on the Google search results page without visiting any external website. With AI Overviews, the rate of zero-click searches has accelerated significantly. For informational queries — which historically drove enormous traffic to blogs and service pages — businesses are seeing measurable declines in organic visits.

Industry Data: Research across multiple sectors indicates that pages ranking for informational queries have seen organic click-through rates decline since the broader rollout of AI Overviews. Websites relying predominantly on generic “what is” and “how to” content are most exposed.

The Brand Citation Opportunity

However, there is a significant counter-opportunity that strategic marketers are already capitalising on. When Google’s AI cites your website as a source within its overview, your brand name appears prominently — often before any traditional search results. This is called AI visibility, and it represents an entirely new form of brand exposure.

Users who then click through from an AI citation are demonstrably higher-intent visitors. They have already received background context and are seeking your specific perspective or service. Conversion rates from AI-referred traffic tend to be substantially higher than average organic traffic.

04. EEAT — Google’s Framework for Content Quality

EEAT is not a new concept, but its importance has been dramatically amplified by AI search. It stands for Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, and Trustworthiness, and it is the core framework Google uses to evaluate whether content is worthy of featuring in AI-generated responses.

Experience — First-Person Credibility

Google now actively looks for evidence that the content creator has direct, personal experience with the subject matter. Have you personally implemented the strategy you are describing? Have you used the product you are reviewing? Have you worked in the industry you are writing about? AI can aggregate public knowledge — it cannot replicate lived, specific experience. This is your primary competitive advantage over generic content.

Expertise — Demonstrable Professional Knowledge

Expertise requires that your knowledge be verifiably deep, current, and accurate. It is supported through professional credentials, real-world application, subject-specific depth, and well-reasoned analysis. In the AI era, generic content written with broad language and no deeper signal is increasingly easy for Google to deprioritise.

Authoritativeness — Third-Party Validation

Authority cannot be self-declared — it must be earned through external recognition. This includes quality backlinks from respected industry sources, mentions in reputable publications, citations by other professionals, and a demonstrable track record of client results. Authority accumulates over time and is one of the most durable competitive moats you can build in SEO.

Trustworthiness — Transparency and Accuracy

Trust is the foundation on which the other three elements rest. It encompasses website security (HTTPS), transparent authorship and organisational information, accurate and well-sourced content, clear privacy and editorial policies, and positive external reviews. Google’s quality raters specifically look for signals that a website is operated by a legitimate, identifiable entity that takes responsibility for its content.

The EEAT competitive advantage: AI-generated content at scale, without genuine human expertise behind it, fails Google’s quality standards. This means that businesses and professionals who have built authentic expertise and can demonstrate it consistently are positioned to win in the AI search era — not lose to it.

05. Who Wins and Who Gets Left Behind

Positioned to Win

  • Professionals and specialists writing authoritatively in their own field — doctors, legal practitioners, financial advisors, engineers, and digital marketing strategists.
  • Businesses with strong local signals — verified Google Business Profiles, positive reviews, active local citations.
  • Content creators who publish original research, proprietary data, or documented case studies from real client work.
  • Brands with multi-platform authority — positive presence across social media, review platforms, and industry forums.

At Significant Risk

  • Websites publishing high volumes of AI-generated content without genuine human oversight, expertise, or editorial value.
  • Anonymous or low-transparency sites lacking clear authorship, contact information, or organisational details.
  • Content strategies built on keyword density rather than genuine value — thin articles that repeat widely available information without adding perspective.

06. 7 Professional Steps to Dominate AI-Era SEO

The following is not theoretical advice. These are the specific strategic actions I implement with clients to improve their AI search visibility and organic performance.

Establish Comprehensive Author Authority

Every content page must carry a detailed author profile: full name, professional credentials, relevant experience, profile photograph, and links to professional profiles. Implement Schema markup for Person and Author entities. This allows Google’s systems to verify and index your expertise as a structured signal, not just text on a page.

Build Topical Authority Through Content Clusters

Rather than publishing isolated articles, develop comprehensive topic clusters. Create a definitive pillar page on your core subject, supported by detailed sub-topic articles that link back to the central resource.

Optimise for Direct Answer Formats

Structure content to directly answer the user’s query within the first 100 words. Use clear question-based headings, direct definitions, and structured lists for multi-step processes.

Implement Advanced Schema Markup

Deploy structured data markup across Article, FAQPage, HowTo, LocalBusiness, Review, and Person schemas where applicable. Structured data gives Google explicit context about your content.

Invest in Brand Signals Across Platforms

Google evaluates your brand beyond your website. Actively manage your Google Business Profile, earn reviews, maintain a professional LinkedIn presence, and engage authentically across industry channels.

Develop Proprietary Data and Original Research

Content with original research, proprietary data, or documented case studies is far more valuable in the AI era. Your original findings become uniquely citable and much harder to replace.

Integrate Video and Multi-Format Content

Google is increasingly incorporating video content — particularly from YouTube — into AI-generated responses. A video strategy strengthens your authority across both web and video search.

Priority Action: If you take only one step this week, invest in Steps 1 and 3. Establishing clear author authority and structuring your existing content to answer queries directly are the highest-leverage, lowest-cost actions available to any business right now.

07. What’s Coming in 2025 and Beyond

The trajectory of AI search points consistently in one direction: greater integration, broader coverage, and higher quality thresholds. Here is what forward-looking businesses need to anticipate:

Expanded AI Overview Coverage

Google has clearly signalled its intent to expand AI Overviews to cover a significantly larger share of queries. Categories that were previously insulated — complex B2B queries, local service searches, transactional intent searches — are progressively being included. The window for adapting before your category is affected is narrowing.

Entity-Based Search Dominance

Google’s Knowledge Graph is becoming increasingly central to how AI search evaluates authority. Brands, individuals, and organisations that are well-represented as verified entities in Google’s Knowledge Graph receive preferential treatment in AI responses. Establishing your entity — through structured data, Wikipedia presence where relevant, and consistent NAP data — is becoming a foundational SEO requirement.

The Convergence of SEO and Brand Marketing

The distinction between SEO and brand reputation management is collapsing. Google’s AI evaluates your brand holistically — across reviews, social media, press mentions, and third-party assessments. Businesses that treat SEO as purely technical and separate from brand strategy will consistently underperform those that understand it as an integrated discipline.

My Assessment for Kannur Businesses

For local businesses in Kannur and the broader Kerala market, AI search represents both a risk and a significant opportunity. The businesses that establish genuine digital authority now — through strategic SEO, professional content, and strong local signals — will be disproportionately visible as AI search coverage expands to local and regional queries.

The investment required to build this authority is not trivial, but the competitive advantage it creates is durable. Businesses that act in the next 12 months will establish positions that will be extremely difficult for later entrants to displace.

08. Final Thoughts from Safwan PP

SEO has not become more difficult — it has become more honest. The strategies that are rewarded in the AI search era are, fundamentally, the strategies that should always have been rewarded: genuine expertise, helpful content, transparent authorship, and real value delivered to real people.

What has changed is that the bar for visibility is higher, the measurement is more sophisticated, and the consequences of cutting corners are more severe. For businesses serious about sustainable digital growth, this is a clarifying moment — an opportunity to differentiate on the basis of genuine quality rather than technical tricks.
